abstract
Molten carbonate salts as phase change materials have received particular attention for high-
temperature thermal energy storage and heat transfer applications due to desirable thermal characteris-
tics such as wide operating temperature range, low causticity and excellent thermal stability. In this
study, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed on molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 based
on an effective pair potential model, a Born-Mayer type combined with a Coulomb term. The temperature
dependences of thermodynamic properties including the density, thermal expansion coefﬁcient, speciﬁc
heat capacity, shear viscosity, thermal conductivity and ion self-diffusion coefﬁcient were simulated in
detail from 1150 to 1500 K, which was all difﬁcult to achieve from experiments on account of high-
temperature extreme conditions. The simulation results were in satisfactory agreement with experimen-
tal data available with high calculation accuracies. In particular, both the equilibrium molecular dynam-
ics (EMD) and non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (NEMD) simulations were tried to calculate the shear
viscosity and thermal conductivity, and the NEMD method turned out to be more suitable for molten
alkali carbonate salts with higher calculation accuracies of 15.11% and 22.48% for shear viscosity and
thermal conductivity separately. Moreover, the radial distribution functions (RDF) and coordination
number curves of the molten salt were characterized to explore the temperature dependences of macro-
scopic properties from microscopic view, and the results suggested that the changes of thermodynamic
properties with temperature were induced by the distance changes between Na2CO3 particles. Besides, it
could be concluded that the structure of CO3 2/C0 was inferred reasonably to be ortho-triangular pyramid
from the comprehensive analysis of local structures including the angular distribution functions (ADF).

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of global economy, enormous
energy consumption has brought about serious energy shortage
and environment pollution. Renewable energy, such as solar
energy, has been receiving extensive attentions worldwide [1,2].
Developing efﬁcient energy storage and heat transfer technology
for concentrating solar energy is required urgently due to its
intermittence and instability [3–6]. Molten salts have been studied
extensively for thermal energy storage and heat transfer applica-
tions due to desirable thermal characteristics such as wide operat-
ing temperature range, low vapor pressure, moderate heat
capacity, low viscosity and excellent thermal stability [7–10].
Generally, the molten salts include chlorides, ﬂuorides, carbonates
and nitrates, among which nitrate salts have been employed
widely as thermal energy storage media for concentrating solar
power plants because of their low melting point and high thermal
stability below 773 K [11–15]. However, molten nitrate salts are
limited to low and medium temperature thermal energy storage
applications, and no longer adequate to meet the demand for
higher heating temperature in industry. Therefore, a novel molten
salt with low melting, high operating temperature and excellent
thermal stability is desired worldwide [16–18].
Molten carbonate salts have received particular attentions for
high-temperature thermal energy storage and heat transfer appli-
cations owing to their excellent characteristics of wide working
temperature range, low causticity, low unit cost and excellent
thermal stability up to 1273 K [16,17]. However, it’s because of
the high working temperature of molten carbonate salts that rele-
vant properties such as shear viscosity and thermal conductivity
are difﬁcult, if not impossible, to be obtained accurately due to
the limitation of extreme test conditions, which are of great signif-
icance for evaluating their potential in practical applications.

Fortunately, computer simulations can be a complementary tool
for predicting the macroscopic and microscopic properties of ionic
crystal materials, among which molecular dynamics (MD) simula-
tion has proved its value for the study of thermodynamic proper-
ties and local structures of molten salts [19]. Dixon and Sangster
[20–22] simulated the radial distribution functions (RDF), velocity
autocorrelation functions (ACF) and mean square displacements
(MSD) of a series of pure molten alkali halides (RbF, RbCl, RbBr,
RbI, CsF, CsCl, CsBr and CsI) by MD simulations using the Born-
Mayer-Huggins potential model, and veriﬁed the suitability of
the model for molten alkali halides. Galamba et al. [23–26] calcu-
lated the shear viscosity and thermal conductivity of pure molten
NaCl and KCl through the equilibrium molecular dynamics (EMD)
and non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (NEMD) simulations
within the framework of the Born-Mayer-Huggins ionic potential,
and the simulation results of the two molecular dynamics simula-
tion methods were all much overestimated but in agreement with
experimental data in terms of the development trend with temper-
ature. Besides, the NEMD simulation behaved better than the EMD
simulation. Wang et al. [27,28] performed MD simulations on a
series of molten alkali halides (LiCl, NaCl, KCl, RbCl, CsCl, LiCl-
NaCl, LiCl-KCl and NaCl-KCl) with the veriﬁed Born-Mayer-
Huggins pair potential. The systematic properties such as the den-
sity, shear viscosity, thermal conductivity, ion self-diffusion coefﬁ-
cient, the RDF and angular distribution functions (ADF) were all
presented in detail, and the effects of temperature and cation size
on the thermodynamic properties and local structures were dis-
cussed. Tissen and Janssen [29] reported the MD simulations on
molten carbonates Li2CO3,Na2CO3, and K2CO3 in the NVT ensemble
at 1200 K. A signiﬁcant change in structure of the melt was
observed on going from Li2CO3 to K2CO3, and rotation of the
CO3 2/C0 was substantially easier in K2CO3 than in Li2CO3. Also, the cal-
culated RDF, velocity ACF and MSD were in good agreement with
the experimental data available. However, of all the previous MD
simulations, most investigations focused on the molten alkali
halides, but few studies worked on molten carbonate salts, espe-
cially the macroscopic properties combined with microscopic
structures.
In this paper, thermodynamic properties and local structures of
molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 in liquid state were systematically
reported by means of MD simulations with an effective pair poten-
tial model, a Born-Mayer type combined with a Coulomb term.
Thermodynamic properties including the density, thermal expan-
sion coefﬁcient, speciﬁc heat capacity, shear viscosity, thermal con-
ductivity and ion self-diffusion coefﬁcient were calculated from
1150 to 1500 K, and their temperature dependences were also
investigated in detail. It should be emphasized that the shear vis-
cosity and thermal conductivity were simulated through the
EMD and NEMD simulations in this study, and the better one of
the two molecular dynamics simulation methods was determined
for the MD simulations on molten alkali carbonates in terms of cal-
culation accuracy. Moreover, the RDF and coordination number
curves of the molten salt were characterized to analyse the tem-
perature dependences of macroscopic properties from microscopic
view. Besides, the ADF was simulated to study the structure of
CO3 2/C0 combined with the RDF and coordination number
simulations.

2.2. Simulation details
All the MD simulations on molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 are
performed using the open source molecular simulation package
LAMMPS from 1150 to 1500 K. The MD simulations reported here
are performed on a simulation system consisting of 9216 atoms,
and the numbers of Na, C and O are distributed as the proportion
of 2:1:3. The initial crystal cell is generated by parameters of solid
state at 800 K provided by Swainson, Dove and Harris [33], and
then expands to the simulation box by supercell method. The peri-
odic boundary conditions are adopted to keep the number of par-
ticles constant in simulation system and eliminate the boundary
effect. The cut-off distance of short-range interactions is set to be
20 Å, which is almost equal to half of the smallest side length of
the simulation box after relaxation. While the long-range interac-
tion calculations are computed in K-space using the PPPM solver
[34] to eliminate the truncation errors, and the accuracy of calcu-
lated forces is 1.0 /C2 10/C06. The initial velocities are randomly
assigned and obeyed the Gaussian distribution. The Verlet algo-
rithm is used to solve Newton’s equations of motion with a time
step of 1.0 fs.
In the MD simulations, the simulation system is ﬁrst heated to
1500 K and then cools to desired temperatures from 1150 to
1500 K with an interval of 50 K. After that, the system is sufﬁ-
ciently equilibrated in the NPT ensemble for 2 million time steps
at corresponding temperature with the atmospheric pressure,
and the temperature and stress damping parameters are set to
be 100 and 500 fs separately. Subsequently, the system is in NVT
ensemble lasting for at least 5 million timesteps, even up to 15 mil-
lion timesteps, to ensure good statistics for the computation of
desired properties and structures such as the shear viscosity and
thermal conductivity.

3.2. Local structures
3.2.1. Radial distribution functions and coordination number curves
In order to ﬁnd the temperature dependences of thermody-
namic properties of molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 in liquid state
from the microscopic view, MD simulations on local structures
were performed from 1150 to 1450 K, with the results of the RDF
and coordination number curves within the cut-off distance of
short-range interactions. In addition, the local structures in solid
state were also simulated at 300 K to discover structure changes
with temperature in liquid state.
Fig. 15 shows the RDF and coordination number changes of the
pairs C-Na, Na-Na, Na-O, C-C, C-O and O-O with temperature for
describing the local structures. As shown in the ﬁgure, the RDF
curves in liquid state all tends rapidly to be unity after the second
neighbour peak, indicating the short-range order and long-range
disorder as expected in liquid state. The RDF of unlike-atom pair
C-Na is shown in Fig. 15(a), and the position rmax of the ﬁrst neigh-
bour peak almost has no change with temperature in liquid state,
meaning that the distance between Na+ and CO3 2/C0 remains the
same. While in Fig. 15(b) and (d), the two ﬁrst neighbour peaks
for the like-atom of pairs Na-Na and C-C shifts to right with tem-
perature in the RDF curves, which indicates that the distances
between cations Na+ and anions CO3 2/C0 all increases when the tem-
perature is increasing. In terms of the coordination number of liq-
uid molten Na2CO3, the coordination numbers of the pairs
decreases sharply from solid to liquid state and changes slightly
in liquid state with temperature. It is concluded that, the increas-
ing distance between Na2CO3 particles with temperature increases
the molar volume and diminishes the ability of resistance to shear
deformation and heat transfer by vibration between ions, which
accounts for the temperature dependences of thermodynamic
properties such as shear viscosity and thermal conductivity of liq-
uid molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 from the microscopic view.
In addition, as shown in Fig. 15(e) and (f) for the pairs C-O and
O-O, the positions rmax of the two ﬁrst neighbour peaks have
almost no change and the width of the two ﬁrst neighbour peaks
all increases with temperature in the RDF curves, which indicates
that both the covalent bond C-O stretching vibration and the
out-of-plane bending vibration based on C atom of CO3 2/C0 become
strong with temperature, meaning that the bond energy in the
covalent bond C-O and the kinetic energy in CO3 2/C0 increases with
temperature. Besides, the coordination numbers N(rmin) of the
pairs C-O and O-O are determined to be 3.00 and 2.00 separately
from the coordination number curves in Fig. 15(e) and (f). Since
the distance between atoms C and O is evaluated as 1.17 Å and
the distance between atoms O and O is 1.00 Å from the RDF anal-

4. Conclusions
In this paper, thermodynamic properties and local structures of
molten alkali carbonate Na2CO3 from 1150 to 1500 K were simu-
lated by means of MD simulations with an effective pair potential
model, a Born-Mayer type combined with a Coulomb term. The
simulated thermodynamic properties including the density, ther-
mal expansion coefﬁcient, speciﬁc heat capacity, shear viscosity,
thermal conductivity and ion self-diffusion coefﬁcient were all in
satisfactory agreement with the experimental data available. In
particular, compared with the EMD simulations, the NEMD simula-
tion method turned out to be more efﬁcient for calculating the
shear viscosity and thermal conductivity of molten alkali carbonate
Na2CO3 in terms of calculation accuracy, and the simulation errors
of shear viscosity and thermal conductivity were 15.11% and
22.48%, respectively. From the MD simulation results of local struc-
tures, the increasing distance between Na2CO3 particles with tem-
perature increased the molar volume and diminished the ability of
resistance to shear deformation and heat transfer by vibration
between ions, accounting for the temperature dependences of
thermodynamic properties of liquid molten alkali carbonate Na2-
CO3 from microscopic view. Moreover, the atom C was three-
coordinated by atom O, and the structure of CO3 2/C0 was inferred rea-
sonably to be ortho-triangular pyramid. The accuracy and reliabil-
ity of MD simulation method are veriﬁed for study of local
structures and thermodynamic properties on molten alkali carbon-
ates, and this method can be used in future work on other molten
carbonate salts and their mixtures.
